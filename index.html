It is a real WordPress site with sixteen published pages, a separate page for each of your three services, a service-areas section, a certifications page, a reviews page and fifty-nine blog posts. Most operators in your trade have four pages and no blog at all.

Finding 1 — You Ran Meta Ads Last Winter With No Meta Pixel
What is installed on your site today: Google Analytics 4 G-LNMF7NBGBL present Google Ads tag AW-730405747 present Meta pixel not found Google Tag Manager not found (so nothing is hidden inside a container) Only Facebook references on the site: facebook.com/EasyBreezyPros/ a link to your page, not a pixel
This is why last winter reads as “it helped a little bit”

Without a pixel, three things are true at once, and all three matter for what you are asking:

  • You cannot tell what it did. Meta can report clicks, but it cannot connect a click to a booked job on your site. “A little bit” is the most precise answer anyone could have given you.
  • You cannot retarget. Everybody who visited your site last winter and did not call is the cheapest audience you will ever have access to, and it was never collected. That audience does not exist now and cannot be recovered retroactively.
  • You cannot repeat it. If last winter worked, nobody can say which part worked, so this winter starts from zero again.

This is the one technical item. It is a tag, not a redesign. It changes nothing a visitor sees.

We checked the served HTML for fbq(, connect.facebook.net and any tag-manager container. A static read cannot prove absence with total certainty — but with no container present there is nowhere for it to be hiding, and whoever runs your ads can confirm it in about a minute.

Finding 2 — Winter Is Not Your Off-Season. It Is Your Unworked Season.
Your three services do not share a calendar
  • Carpet cleaning is a spring and summer service. People clean carpets before guests arrive, after winter, when they can open windows. Your own blog knows this — you published “Carpet Cleaning Before Summer Guests Arrive” in June.
  • Air duct cleaning is a winter service. The furnace runs, windows stay shut, and the air in the house gets recirculated for four months. Indoor air quality stops being abstract in December.
  • Dryer vent cleaning peaks in January. Not by opinion — by federal fire data. More clothes, heavier fabrics, nothing dried outdoors.

If winter feels slow, it is because the winter services are being marketed with the summer ones, in a single undifferentiated “air duct and carpet cleaning” message, all year round.

You are not fighting seasonality. You are running a summer offer in January.

Finding 4 — The Real Answer To “Consistent” Is Not Ads. It Is Contracts.
Your own website already claims this, and then does nothing with it

Your service-areas page says you are “trusted by property managers and home builders.” Your services page offers “Services for Both Residential & Commercial Properties” with Commercial options in the menu for both duct and carpet cleaning.

And you have four commercial posts already written: Commercial Air Duct Cleaning Cedar City · Commercial Warehouse Duct Cleaning · Duct Cleaning For Municipal And Commercial Buildings · Sick Employees Cost Businesses A Lot. Plus two for realtors.

Pages published on your site: 16 Pages for commercial or property managers: 0 /air-duct-cleaning-in-cedar-city-utah/ residential /carpet-cleaning-in-cedar-city-utah/ residential /dryer-vent-cleaning-in-cedar-city-utah/ residential
This is the difference between a busy winter and a quiet one

A homeowner books when they feel like it. A property manager books on a schedule. Apartment blocks, rentals, HOAs, motels, offices and municipal buildings turn over units and service buildings on calendars that do not care what the weather is doing — and in Cedar City a great deal of that is student and seasonal rental stock that turns over in winter.

Consumer ads make a good month. Commercial and property-manager accounts make a consistent year, because the same building calls you again next year without an ad being served.

You are already selling it, already trusted for it, and have written six pieces about it. There is nowhere on your website to send anybody who wants it.

Every angle above maps to a service you already sell and, in six cases, to a blog post you have already written and published.
What We Would Do, In Order
  • Install and verify the Meta pixel. One tag. No design change. Without it nothing else this winter can be measured or retargeted, and you will be having this same conversation next September.
  • Build the commercial and property-manager page. A new page, not a change to an existing one. This is the consistency lever and everything else is a good month.
  • Split the seasons in the advertising. Dryer vent and duct cleaning from October. Carpet from March. Stop running one blended message across a calendar it does not fit.
  • Promote the six winter posts you already own, in the eight weeks each is relevant, with retargeting behind them.
  • Repeat the St George page for Hurricane and Washington, and for dryer vents and carpet, not only ducts.
  • Then judge it — on booked jobs per month December to March against last winter, which is a comparison you cannot currently make.

You asked how to stay consistent through winter. You already sell the service that peaks in January.

It needs a pixel underneath it, a page for the commercial buyers, and a calendar that matches your own trade.
