The services page is the thin one, and it is the one that matters

Your services page is 330 words and it has to carry all three services. It is the page a visitor lands on when they are deciding what they need, and it is the second shortest page on the site.

Your individual service pages are fine — 600 to 800 words each. It is the hub above them that is thin, which is a short job, not a rebuild.

Finding 2 — No LocalBusiness Markup
Structured data found: Organization WebSite Structured data missing: LocalBusiness the one that matters for a local service firm Service what you do FAQPage answers Google can quote directly AggregateRating the markup that puts stars in search results
This is the cheapest technical item on the list

Organization markup tells Google you are a company. LocalBusiness markup tells it you are a company at a place, serving an area, with opening hours. For a firm whose customers search “near me”, that is the difference that matters.

And AggregateRating is what puts a star rating beside your listing in search results. You are “top-rated by customers” by your own description and none of that is marked up anywhere.

This is a one-off job measured in hours, it changes nothing a visitor sees, and it does not touch the design you are happy with.

Finding 3 — Two Phone Numbers
(435) 334-6359 (435) 890-3355 Both published on the site. Neither is call-tracked.
Worth a decision, not necessarily a change

Two numbers is fine if there is a reason for it — one for the office and one for you, say. It becomes a problem in three places: Google Business Profile wants one primary number, directory listings drift apart over time, and neither number is tracked, so a call that came from an advert is indistinguishable from one that came from a van.

You run Meta ads and Local Services Ads. In this trade most enquiries are calls. Without call tracking, the channel that produces your phone calls cannot be identified — which means the Google Ads tag you already have is only counting part of the picture.

Finding 4 — The One From The Winter Plan, Restated
Google Analytics 4 present Google Ads tag present Meta pixel not found Google Tag Manager not found (nowhere for one to hide)
It belongs in the technical audit as well, because it is a technical fact

You told Paige you added Meta ads last winter and they helped a little. There is no Meta pixel on this website, and no tag manager that could be concealing one.

So that campaign could not be measured, and nobody who visited during it was collected for retargeting. It is a tag. It is not a design change, and it does not touch anything you like about the site.

A static read cannot prove absence with certainty. Whoever runs your ads can confirm it in a minute, and it is worth doing before you spend again this winter.

If You Only Did Four Things
  • Put the Meta pixel on — before you spend anything on Meta this winter.
  • Add LocalBusiness and Service markup, and rating markup once you are collecting reviews. Hours, not days.
  • Decide which number is the business number, and put call tracking on it so phone enquiries can be attributed.
  • Take the services page from 330 words to something that actually sells three services — and while you are in there, that is where a commercial section would go.
